# Sitelinks

## Definition

Sitelinks are additional navigation links displayed beneath the main organic result for branded or domain-specific queries, providing users with shortcuts to commonly visited sections of the target website. Google algorithmically generates and selects these links — they cannot be manually configured or added by publishers.

## Description

When Google has high confidence that a user's query is directed at a specific website (branded queries, exact domain names), it supplements the standard result with Sitelinks: up to 6 sub-page links with brief descriptive text. These links typically point to the most valuable or frequently accessed pages on the site — "About," "Products," "Pricing," "Contact," "Login," "Blog," or category pages.

Sitelinks dramatically improve the utility of branded SERP results, allowing users to navigate directly to their intended destination without an intermediate click. They also substantially increase the visual footprint of the main result in the SERP, often pushing all competitor results below the fold.

Google automatically selects Sitelink pages based on internal link prominence (which pages receive the most internal links from the homepage/main navigation), anchor text clarity, page crawl depth, and estimated user utility. Publishers formerly had the ability to "demote" unwanted Sitelinks via Google Search Console, but this feature was removed in 2016 — Google now makes all Sitelink selection decisions unilaterally.

The **Sitelinks Searchbox** is a distinct variant requiring `WebSite` schema with `SearchAction` markup, embedding a site-specific search field directly in the SERP result.

## Key Attributes

**Trigger Conditions:** Branded queries (company name, product name), exact domain queries. Not triggered for generic keyword queries — only when Google has strong navigational confidence.

**Control:** None for standard Sitelinks. `WebSite` schema with `SearchAction` enables (but does not guarantee) the Sitelinks Searchbox variant.

## Optimization Relevance

Since Sitelinks cannot be configured directly, optimization means ensuring clear site architecture and internal linking so Google can confidently identify the most important pages. A clear primary navigation, consistent internal link text, and distinct page titles are the key signals. Ensuring the homepage's internal links cover the most strategically important sub-pages increases the likelihood of those pages appearing as Sitelinks.

## Status & Evolution

**Current Status:** Active. Demotion feature removed 2016.
